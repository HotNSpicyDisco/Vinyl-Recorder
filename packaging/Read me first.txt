VINYL RECORDER
Record your records, look them up on Discogs, split them into tracks and save tagged FLAC/MP3 files.
Windows 10 or 11, 64-bit.


GETTING STARTED

1. Unzip this folder somewhere it can stay, e.g. Documents\Vinyl Recorder
   (not inside the zip, and not Downloads if you clean that out).

2. Double-click "Vinyl Recorder.exe".
   Windows may say "Windows protected your PC" because the app isn't from the Microsoft Store:
   click "More info", then "Run anyway". It only asks the first time.

3. It offers to add itself to the Start menu and desktop. Say Yes.

4. Connect it to your Discogs account (free):
   - On discogs.com go to Settings > Developers > "Generate new token"
   - In Vinyl Recorder: File > Settings, paste the token, OK.
   Your collection then appears on the Album tab.


SETTING UP YOUR AUDIO

On the Record tab:
- Device: pick your audio interface. The "ASIO" entry is best if your interface has one
  (Focusrite, Behringer, etc. - install the maker's driver first).
- Left / Right input: the input numbers your turntable or mixer is plugged into
  (e.g. 1 & 2, or 3 & 4 on a Scarlett 4i4).
- Press Apply, play a record, and set your mixer so peaks reach about -6 dB
  and the clip counter stays at 0.
- If the device won't open, close other audio apps (DJ software, DAWs) and press Apply again.


HOW IT WORKS

1. Album:  pick the record from your collection (or search Discogs).
2. Record: "Whole LP" records side A, the flip, and side B in one go. Press Stop at the end.
3. Split & save: tracks are found automatically. Scroll to zoom, drag lines to adjust,
   Space to play/stop. Choose FLAC and/or MP3, then Save.

Music is saved to Music\Vinyl Rips (change it in File > Settings).
Raw recordings are kept in Music\Vinyl Rips\_recordings - delete them when you're happy.
